# Product Rules

## Collection

- The user's saved Spotify albums form the primary collection.
- The shelf is the application's home screen.
- Albums are organized into record-store-style bins.
- The listener manually fingers through overlapping, front-facing sleeves.
- There is no text search.

## Album selection

- The album—not the individual song—is the primary unit.
- A centered album rises slightly from the stack.
- Selecting it pulls the album fully out of its bin.
- The track listing is informational, not a collection of play buttons.
- The album can be returned to its exact position in the bin.

## Playback

- Selecting a new album always starts it from the beginning.
- Songs play in the album's original order.
- Shuffle is unavailable.
- Conventional next-track and previous-track buttons are unavailable.
- The listener may pause or stop at any time.
- The listener may stop the current album and choose another.
- Playback stops when the album ends.
- Unrelated music does not begin automatically.

## Seeking

- Seeking is allowed.
- Seeking should resemble repositioning a record player's needle.
- The record represents one continuous album timeline.
- Moving across track boundaries is possible without turning tracks into
  individually selected items.

## Experience principles

- Browsing should feel spatial rather than query-based.
- Friction should encourage discovery without causing confusion.
- The interface should favor album artwork over metadata.
- Physical metaphors should remain understandable on a touchscreen.
- Accessibility must not depend exclusively on gestures or animation.

## Not part of the initial prototype

- Recommendations
- Social features
- Custom bins
- Listening statistics
- Ratings or reviews
- A database
- Real Spotify authentication or playback
